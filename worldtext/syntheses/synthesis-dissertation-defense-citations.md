## I. Enters Field

The dissertation positions itself at the intersection of media theory, human-computer interaction (HCI), AI ethics, and practice-based design research.

### 1. Digital Media & Media Studies
*   **The Claim:** Generative AI is not merely an automation utility; it is a new class of world-forming media.
*   **Defense & Citation:** We extend W. J. T. Mitchell’s (**Mitchell 1994**, *Picture Theory*) concept of the **imagetext**—the inescapable friction and merging of visual and verbal representations. We argue that generative AI represents a phase shift from the static *imagetext* to the dynamic, computational **worldtext** where language becomes executable space.

### 2. HCI / Design Research & Practice-Based Research
*   **The Claim:** Creative prompting is an act of design inquiry, not a mechanical routine.
*   **Defense & Citation:** We align with Research through Design (RtD) (**Zimmerman, Forlizzi, &amp; Evenson 2007**; **Gaver 2012**) and agential interaction design (**Karpathy 2026**). The practice of prompting is treated as a steering behavior within highly viscous, high-dimensional statistical spaces.

### 3. Narrative Theory & Cybernetics
*   **The Claim:** Human-AI storytelling is a cybernetic feedback loop governed by statistical distributions.
*   **Defense & Citation:** We read the prompt-output-revision loop through cybernetic systems theory (**Wiener 1948**; **Beer 1972**) and ergodic literature (**Aarseth 1997**, *Cybertext*), where narrative construction is a process of navigating and traversing a text-space under agential constraints.

## II. Responds to Limit

The dissertation confronts six critical limits in current academic and industry discourse.

### Limit 1: Prompting treated as mere technique
*   **The Gap:** Prompting is widely reduced to a transient "productivity hack" or "job skill" (e.g., prompt engineering).
*   **Defense & Citation:** We intervene by conceptualizing prompting as a cultural, narrative, and epistemic practice. As Oppenlaender (**Oppenlaender 2024**) argues, prompt craft is a socio-technical art form. We provide a systematic methodology (**thick prompting**) that anchors it in interpretive science.

### Limit 2: AI outputs treated as isolated content
*   **The Gap:** Discourse focuses on individual outputs (e.g., "hallucinations" or single images) rather than the systemic ecology of their production.
*   **Defense & Citation:** Treating outputs in isolation ignores the statistical gravity of the model defaults. We cite Hintze et al. (**Hintze, Proschinger Åström, &amp; Schossau 2026**) on how unguided models converge toward a generic "platform mean" (the twelve lighthouses). The output is only legible when mapped against the entire prompt-output-revision path.

### Limit 3: Authorship debates stuck on human vs. machine
*   **The Gap:** Debates are trapped in a binary: either the human is the sole author or the machine is an autonomous creator.
*   **Defense & Citation:** Following Lucy Suchman’s critique of plans and situated action (**Suchman 1987, 2007**), we frame authorship as distributed agential performance. The prompt is not a command; it is an active constraint that navigates a pre-existing statistical distribution (**viscosity**).

### Limit 4: AI ethics reduced to bias, hallucination, and consent
*   **The Gap:** Ethical concerns are isolated to dataset bias, model hallucinations, and training data consent.
*   **Defense & Citation:** We expand AI ethics by examining the politics of the generated world itself. We draw on Hannes Bajohr’s (**Bajohr 2024**) analysis of "platform realism" and postcolonial critiques of representation (**Spivak 1988**) to ask what worlds are made default, what values are stabilized, and what options are silenced by latent-space architectures.

### Limit 5: Media theory lacking vocabulary for executable description
*   **The Gap:** Traditional media theory lacks a vocabulary for description that actively compiles space and state.
*   **Defense & Citation:** Classic ekphrasis theory—from James Heffernan (**Heffernan 1993**) to Spitzer (**Spitzer 1955**)—narrowed the definition to "verbal representation of visual representation." We recover the ancient, classical rhetorical definition of ekphrasis (**Webb 1999, 2009**) as `logos periegematikos`—vivid speech that actively *produces* sight in the listener.

### Limit 6: Design research lacking thick methods for generative worldmaking
*   **The Gap:** HCI lacks qualitative, autoethnographic methods to study how designers iteratively navigate latent distributions.
*   **Defense & Citation:** We introduce Geertzian thick description (**Geertz 1973**) to design research. Thick prompting provides a six-layer documentation protocol to capture not just the code of the prompt, but the stakes, audience, native intent, and reclassification cascades of the generation loop.

## III. Intervenes Through Method / Practice / Argument

The core of our intervention is a theory-method pair that bridges media theory and agential design.

### 1. Operative Description & Operative Ekphrasis (The Theory)
*   **Defense:** In generative systems, description is no longer passive representation; it is the control signal that navigates latent space. 
*   **Citation:** We build on Hannes Bajohr’s (**Bajohr 2024**, *Operative Ekphrasis*) argument that in generative systems, describing an object is structurally identical to producing it. We ground this genealogy in Homer's Shield of Achilles (*Iliad* XVIII), showing that the ancient precursor of ekphrasis was already a world-compilation instruction set.

### 2. Thick Prompting (The Method)
*   **Defense:** We map Geertz’s (**Geertz 1973**) layers of interpretive description onto prompt craft, creating a repeatable research method.
*   **Citation:** Thick prompting documents the visible tokens (Layer 1), native intent (Layer 2), stylistic codes (Layer 3), targeted audience (Layer 4), functional stakes (Layer 5), and downstream reclassifications (Layer 6). This prevents the "vague vibes" critique of practice-based research by providing empirical, auditable logs of the steering process.

### 3. Worldtext (The Object)
*   **Defense:** The worldtext names the narrative-spatial system compiled from description.
*   **Citation:** We synthesize Mark J.P. Wolf’s (**Wolf 2012**) imaginary-world construction with Espen Aarseth's (**Aarseth 1997**) cybertext theory. A worldtext is not a single output image (imagetext), but a persistent, rule-governed interactive environment compiled from text-models and spatial scene graphs.

## V. Opens Door

The dissertation shifts the boundaries of what is studyable and designable.

### 1. For Scholars: Prompting becomes studyable as cultural practice
*   **Defense:** Prompting shifts from technical optimization to the study of cultural memory and text-visual translation.
*   **Citation:** Scholars can use the framework to trace how language models act as "encyclopedic machines" (**Murray 1997**), translating cultural idioms into visual defaults.

### 2. For HCI Researchers: Design for steering instead of control
*   **Defense:** Designers shift focus from building rigid user interfaces to designing steering protocols within latent distributions.
*   **Citation:** We build on agential co-creation literature (**Liu &amp; Chilton 2023**), shifting the HCI paradigm from "direct manipulation" to "latent steering."

### 3. For AI Ethics: Examining generated worlds, not only outputs
*   **Defense:** Ethics moves beyond checking outputs for bias and starts auditing the structural limits and default atmospheric conditions of generated spaces.
